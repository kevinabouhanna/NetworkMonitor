Usage History

The popover shows today, this week or this month. Usage History keeps every hour behind those figures, per app and per network, for as long as you choose.

It's the first section of the main window. Open it from Open Usage History under Data in the popover, or the four squares beside the popover's gear: both open it on the popover's period. Or choose Usage History… (⌘Y) in the right-click menu, or Usage History in the main window's sidebar.

Using it

  • List, Both or Chart, at the start of the toolbar, as Connections has List, Both or Map (#524): the lists alone, the lists on the left and the chart on the right with its legend under it (the default, side by side as Connections has its list and map, #534), or the chart filling the window. Whichever you choose is remembered. With the chart alone, Show is hidden, as there's no list for it to change.
  • Day, Week, Month or Year, in the toolbar, with ‹ Today › beside it (⌘← and ⌘→ step too), as every section has them. Periods are calendar periods, not rolling windows, so "October" means the same bytes every time you look, the way Screen Time and carriers count. A day shows hours, a week or month shows days, a year shows months. Click a bar to open it.
  • Stacked by network type: Wi-Fi, Hotspot, Ethernet and Other. Each type keeps its colour whatever else is in the period. The legend under the chart gives each type's total, so no figure is readable only by hovering.
  • All, Wi-Fi or Hotspot, at the end of the row under the toolbar, narrows everything to that kind of network: the chart, the totals and the lists. It opens on Hotspot while you're on one, and on All otherwise. Ethernet and Other are under All, and on the Networks list.
  • Each app's bar is split by network type, in the chart's colours, so an app that used both Wi-Fi and a hotspot shows how much went over each. Splits too small to see are left out; the Networks list has the exact figures.
  • The heading names the period, then gives what was used, with what went over metered connections and the daily average as one quieter line. The daily average divides by the days elapsed so far, not by the length of the month. Metered is shown under All, and only when something went over a metered connection.
  • Show: Apps, Networks or Sessions, at the start of that row. Apps are grouped exactly as in the popover. A session is one visit to a network.
  • The lists are the filters. Click a row to see only that app, network or session, and the chart and totals follow; click it again, or its chip at the end of the lists' row (#192), to undo. Filters combine, with each other and with the switch.
  • Search (⌘F), the magnifying glass in the toolbar, finds apps and networks, and shows as a chip like the other filters.
  • Name a network by right-clicking it under Networks and choosing Rename…. The name is used in History and in exports, for every past visit too.
  • Export CSV…, at the bottom, saves the period on screen, with its filter, hour by hour and app by app, in bytes.
  • Keep history for a month, three months, a year, or forever (the default), in Usage History's settings, behind the gear. Shortening it asks first if it would delete anything.
  • Clear History…, there too, deletes it all. The menu bar totals aren't affected.

While Usage History is shown on a period that includes now, it refreshes every 30 seconds.

What counts

History counts the same internet-only figure as the popover. Mirroring a screen, Time Machine to a NAS or a Plex stream from your own server is never part of it. See accuracy for how that figure is worked out.

It starts the moment you install a version that has it (1.4.0 or later). Nothing from before can be recovered: the old totals have no hours in them, and spreading a lump over a day would be inventing data.

Sessions

A session is one visit to a network: from joining it until you leave for another, or until the Mac has been silent for 30 minutes, as it is after a sleep.

  • Going offline isn't a place. A Wi-Fi blip and a rejoin continue the same visit.
  • A session ends at its last traffic, not when the next network appears. A lid closed at the office ends the office visit when it closed.
  • Waking up. The first packets after a wake arrive before macOS has settled which network you're on (the app waits 2 seconds for it to settle). A session opened then stays provisional for 30 seconds and adopts the network that's named in that time, so it doesn't leave a few-kilobyte visit on the network you closed the lid on.
  • Relaunching on the same network within 30 minutes continues the visit.
  • Each session keeps its own metered verdict, read again as it closes, so a hotspot visit stays metered after you've moved on to home Wi-Fi.
  • Updates that got through stay with their visit (#96). A hotspot visit where one did says 2 updates got through under its name, and choosing it lists them: the app, the version once known, and the data used, or up to when only the most it could be is known. Hotspot mode's Got through list only holds the last metered connection; History keeps them under the same retention and Clear History as everything else. Each is kept once, with the visit it was first seen on, even when one connection runs across several visits.

Networks

A network is identified by its router's hardware address, not its name. Two routers with the same Wi-Fi name stay apart, and roaming between access points of one network stays one network. The same router over Wi-Fi and over Ethernet is one network, which is what a data cap cares about.

What it's called:

  • Its Wi-Fi name, once Location is allowed (macOS shares the name only then). Past visits take the name too.
  • Both names for a router whose bands are named apart, such as Home / Home-5G. 2.4 and 5 GHz on one router are one network.
  • Otherwise its type and the last four characters of the router's address, such as Wi-Fi · 3f2a: Ethernet, USB tethering, or Location refused.
  • Networks not told apart, for traffic the app couldn't place on a network, such as everything on macOS 27 before 1.9.7.
  • A name you give it under Networks wins over all of these.

CSV format

Hour,Network,Network Type,Metered,App,Downloaded (bytes),Uploaded (bytes),Update That Got Through
  • One line per app, per hour, per visit. That's everything History holds; a spreadsheet can total it any way it likes, but couldn't split a daily figure apart.
  • Hour is yyyy-MM-dd HH:mm in your time zone.
  • Without an app filter, each hour also has a Not attributed to an app line for the rest of the internet total, so the file adds up to what the window shows.
  • Update That Got Through is filled on the app's line in the hour an update was first seen, such as Slack updated to 4.41, 120 MB (#96). When the app has no line in that hour, the update gets a line of its own with no bytes, so the columns still add up.
  • Fields are quoted only when they need it (RFC 4180). The file is UTF-8.

How it's built

Storage. ~/Library/Application Support/NetworkMonitor/history.sqlite, through the system's own SQLite library, with no third-party dependency. History only grows, and every view is a range query and a sum, which is the wrong shape for a JSON file. WAL journaling with synchronous = NORMAL: a write is an append rather than an fsync, and a crash can lose the last moments but never corrupt the file. A file that can't be opened is moved aside as history.unreadable-<time>.sqlite, never deleted, and a new one started.

Schema (UsageHistory.swift). One row per hour per session, and one per hour per session per app. An hour is the finest grain any view needs, and keeps a year to roughly a hundred thousand rows. The servers each app talked to, for Connections, are kept the same way.

Table Holds Key
sessions network id, type, default label, start, end (last traffic), metered id
apps app key, name, bundle path, whether it's System, parent app key
traffic per hour and session: interface bytes, LAN bytes, attributed app bytes, each way (hour, session_id)
app_traffic per hour, session and app: bytes in and out (hour, session_id, app_key)
servers a server's address; its name column is only read for rows from before #247 id
app_servers each app and server pair, once, with when it was first seen (#111) id
server_traffic per hour, session and app and server: bytes in and out, and connections blocked (#114) (hour, session_id, app_server_id)
server_names per hour, session and app and server: each name the app reached it by, and how many of those connections went through or were blocked (#247) (hour, session_id, app_server_id, name)
list_blocks per hour, session and app: each site a list blocked, the list, and how many times (#250) (hour, session_id, app_key, name, list)
updates each update that got through: the app, its version, the data it used, and the session it was first seen on (#96) (report, source, name)

The raw figures are stored and the internet figure is derived when reading, with the same clamp the popover uses, so the formula lives in one place: MIN(interface, MAX(interface − LAN, apps)) per direction, per hour and session.

Feeding. UsageStore forwards every byte and every network change to UsageHistory, so History and the popover can never disagree about which network a byte used. Reset Now and midnight clear the current connection's counter, never History.

Writing. Batched in memory and written when the usage file is saved (about every 15 seconds, on a network change, on sleep and on quit), in one transaction of upserts that add to existing rows. Synchronous, because quit calls it, and well under a millisecond. It's also flushed before every read, so the current hour is never missing. A failed batch is dropped, not retried, because a database that refuses one write will refuse the next; the current connection's counter is unaffected.

Reading happens on its own serial queue, so stepping through a year never stalls the menu bar. Measured on a generated 400-day database (5.5 MB): a year's per-app totals in 32 ms, hourly totals in 2 ms.

Retention (historyRetention in preferences) prunes whole days, at most once a day and straight away when the setting changes, then reclaims space with incremental_vacuum.

The chart is Swift Charts, stacked by hand so segments get a 2-point gap and only the top of a bar is rounded. The value axis uses round 1024-based steps chosen by the app (Swift Charts' own steps printed "953.7 MB"). The four colours were checked for colour-blind separation in light and dark mode; two of the light ones fall below 3:1 contrast against the window, which is why every figure is also given as text. Hover dims other bars by mixing them toward the background rather than lowering opacity, because the double-drawn top segment would show through.

Tests in UsageHistoryTests.swift cover the internet figure matching the popover, LAN-only traffic never counting, every session rule above, filters, retention, Clear History, the CSV adding up, DST days having 23 or 25 hourly bars, and the axis steps. They use a temporary database and a UTC calendar.

Known limits

  • History begins when you install 1.4.0 or later.
  • On macOS 27 before 1.9.7, every Wi-Fi network, iPhone hotspots included, was recorded as one network (#70). Those visits can't be split after the fact: nothing recorded when the switches happened.
  • The Day/Week/Month/Year choice is remembered while the app runs, not across launches.
  • Which day a week starts on follows your Mac's region settings.
  • The schema version (user_version) is still 1. The two columns added since, a server's name (#128) and blocked connections (#114), are added to an older file when it opens, if they're missing.
