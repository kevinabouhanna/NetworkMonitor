Features

Everything NetworkMonitor does, and where to find it.

The menu bar readout

Two lines, always visible: ↓ download in green over ↑ upload in light grey, in MB/s or Mbps (Settings › Show speeds in). Mbps matches how your line is sold; MB/s matches the data totals. Amounts of data are always in MB and GB, the way data plans are sold. Pick other colours under Settings › Speed colors.

The readout keeps a fixed width, so nothing beside it in the menu bar jumps around as the numbers change. It updates up to twice a second, less often on battery and in Low Power Mode (see battery). While a speed test runs, the readout shows it in cyan, or in another pair of colours if yours come close to cyan, so a test never looks like the live rates.

  • Click it for the popover.
  • Right-click (or Control-click) it for Test Internet Speed, the last result, Block Everything (#198), Usage History… (⌘Y), Connections… (⌘K), Ads and Trackers… (⌘B), Network…, Settings… (⌘,), About NetworkMonitor, Check for Updates…, Uninstall NetworkMonitor… and Quit.

In the Dock instead

Settings › Show NetworkMonitor in chooses where the app lives (#69). It takes effect at once.

  • Menu bar, the default: the readout above, and a Dock icon only while a window is open.

  • Dock: nothing in the menu bar. A Dock icon choice appears under it (#523):

    • Speeds, Dock's default: the whole Dock icon is the live speeds, download over upload, drawn like the app's icon: its shape and lighting, near black in dark mode and near white in light, following the Mac as it switches. Each speed is the icon's arrow and its figure to three significant figures (12.3), as large as fits, with its unit under it, in the speed colors (on light, drawn darker so they read). It updates as the readout would, at most once a second.
    • App icon: NetworkMonitor's own icon, with no speeds.

    Click the icon for the main window, on the section it was last on. Its menu has what the readout's right-click menu has, the Dock's own Quit included; with Speeds it starts with the speeds in words, which is how VoiceOver reads them there. There's no popover in this mode: a speed test that needs your OK on a hotspot asks on the Network page.

  • Both: the readout in the menu bar, and a Dock icon all the time. The same Dock icon choice appears under it, with App icon to begin with, so the Dock icon is the app's until you choose Speeds. Dock and Both each keep their own choice.

There's no choice of neither: there'd be no way back to the app.

The popover

  • Today | Week | Month, at the top, sets the period for every figure under it: today, this week or this month so far, on every network, read from Usage History. The popover opens on the period you chose last.
  • Four buttons: Data, Connections, Ads blocked and Network, each with its figure. Click one to see its view underneath. The colours are the same everywhere in the app: data blue, connections teal, blocked red, hotspots orange.
  • Data lists the Apps or the Networks that used it, biggest first, each with a bar for its share. The apps re-sort live as they overtake each other, and a dot marks apps moving data right now.
    • Helpers fold into their app: Chrome's dozen helper processes are one Google Chrome row.
    • Commands break out beneath the app that started them. Open iTerm2 or VS Code to see the node, git or curl it launched, each with its own figure. More than four become Other (n).
    • System: macOS's own daemons collapse into one row at the bottom. Open it when you're curious.
  • Connections groups who your apps talked to by Companies, Countries or Apps. Each app talking to a server counts once. A company or country opens into its apps, an app into its companies.
  • Ads blocked lists the Sites or the Apps, busiest first. A site opens into the apps that tried it, an app into the sites it tried. While ad blocking is off, it offers Turn On.
  • Network shows the connection the Mac is on, a This is a hotspot switch, on every network because some phones aren't recognised, and the speed test: the last result and Test Speed.
  • Open …, at the bottom of each view, opens that section of the main window on the same period: Open Usage History (⌘Y), Open Connections (⌘K), Open Ads and Trackers (⌘B) or Open Network. The four squares beside the gear do the same, and the gear opens Settings.
  • Lines under the buttons say what needs saying:
    • a hotspot you've joined that the Mac isn't using, with how to switch behind an ⓘ;
    • on a hotspot with Hotspot mode on, an update that downloaded anyway;
    • Everything is blocked, with Turn Off, while Block Everything is on;
    • ad blocking paused, or off on this network;
    • Per-app figures paused, in grey, if macOS's nettop can't be started. NetworkMonitor keeps retrying, and the total keeps counting regardless.

A list holds six rows and part of a seventh, so a cut-off row tells you there's more. It keeps one height whichever grouping you choose. Esc or a click elsewhere closes it.

A total per connection

The popover counts every network. The Network page counts the one you're on: join a different Wi-Fi network or hotspot and its total starts from zero, so what you see is what this connection has used. Open your hotspot somewhere new and you know exactly what it cost. To compare networks over a period, choose Networks under Data in the popover.

  • A dropout and a reconnect to the same network picks up where it left off.
  • It resets at midnight too, for a connection you never leave.
  • It survives restarts, and a relaunch on the same network carries on.
  • Reset Now (Settings › Popover totals) starts the current network's total over.
  • Counting since (Settings › Popover totals) shows when the current total began.

Networks are told apart by their router's hardware address, which needs no permission, and named after their Wi-Fi network. macOS shares the name only with Location permission, which the app asks for once; refused, a network is named like Wi-Fi · 3f2a.

Every hour of every network goes to Usage History.

Internet only

Mirroring to an Apple TV, Time Machine to a NAS, a Plex stream from your own server: these cross your Wi-Fi but cost no internet data, so they're never counted. An app that only talked to your own network doesn't appear at all. See accuracy.

Usage History

Every hour, per app and per network, for as long as you choose. Look back by day, week, month or year; see each visit to a network as a session; narrow everything to one app, one network or every hotspot at once; export it as CSV. See Usage History.

Connections

Which companies your apps send data to, from which apps, in which countries, and to which servers: grouped by company, app or country, narrowed by clicking, searched, read hour by hour on a timeline, and shaded on a world map that pulses to where your apps reach right now. Nothing is looked up online. Block a company, app, country, site or server, or have it ask the first time an app connects. See Connections.

Ads and Trackers

One switch blocks ads, trackers and malware sites in every app. Overview shows what was blocked in the period, on a chart and by site and app, both used as Usage History's are: hover or click a bar, choose or open a row (#536); Log shows each connection live, as the filter decides it. The lists and your own rules are in its settings. See Ads and Trackers.

Speed test

One click runs a fresh test with the tool built into macOS: download, then upload, then ping, jitter, and ping under load, in Mbps or MB/s. If another app was competing for the line, the result names it. On a hotspot it asks first. See Speed test.

Hotspot mode

One switch, on the Network page, stops your apps updating while you're on a mobile hotspot, puts the connection in Low Data Mode, and tells you which apps it can't stop. See Hotspot mode.

The main window

One window with a sidebar: Usage History, Connections, Ads and Trackers and Network, in the popover's order, with Settings pinned at the bottom. Hide the sidebar with its button or View › Hide Sidebar (⌃⌘S). When a new version is out, a card above Settings offers it (Update).

Each section's toolbar starts with the view: List, Both or Chart in Usage History and Ads and Trackers, List, Both or Map in Connections (#524); Both is always the list on the left and the chart or the map on the right (#534). It has the period (Day, Week, Month, Year) in the middle, then ‹ Today ›, a search button that opens into a field (⌘F), and a gear. The gear shows that section's own settings in place, with a back button. Network is about now, so it has only the gear.

Settings

Settings (⌘,), at the bottom of the sidebar, holds what applies to the whole app. What a row means is behind an ⓘ beside it, which appears when you point at the row (#185, #189).

  • Language: the Mac's, or one chosen for the app
  • Start at login
  • Show NetworkMonitor in the Menu bar, the Dock or Both, and with the Dock or Both, Dock icon: Speeds or App icon
  • Show speeds in MB/s or Mbps
  • Wi-Fi names, until they're on
  • Speed colors (the readout's and the Dock icon's): Download, Upload, the speed test's colours, Use Default Colors
  • Popover totals: Counting since, Sampling, Reset Now
  • Check for updates automatically, and Check Now
  • Sections: a row to each section's settings, the same pages its gear shows

About NetworkMonitor, in the right-click menu and the app menu, is the standard About window: the version, links to the website, what's new and the source code, and the licence.

While the main window is open, NetworkMonitor has a Dock icon and a menu, so ⌘W, ⌘,, ⌘Y, ⌘K, ⌘B and ⌘F work and ⌘-Tab can find it. It goes back to living in the menu bar when you close it, unless it's set to show in the Dock or both.

With the main window open behind another app, a click on the menu bar item shows only the popover, and the window stays behind. ⌘-Tab or the Dock icon shows the window on its own.

Staying out of the way

  • No Dock icon, no app switcher entry, no window to manage. Just the menu bar, unless you choose the Dock or both.
  • Starts at login by default, once. Turn it off in Settings and it stays off.
  • Quit stays quit. Quitting ends it for the session without turning off Start at login, and turning that off doesn't quit the running app.
  • Works with the menu bar set to hide automatically: the popover stays open while the bar hides, and the main window stays in front.
